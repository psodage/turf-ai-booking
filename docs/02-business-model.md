# 1. Business Model Overview

Turf AI Booking is a B2B SaaS and AI automation solution designed for football turf businesses.

The primary paying customer is the turf owner or turf business.

The system helps turf owners:

- Automate customer conversations
- Reduce manual booking work
- Reduce fake and unverified bookings
- Automate payment collection
- Prevent double bookings
- Manage bookings through WhatsApp
- Access business information through AI
- Generate reports

Customers who want to book a turf use WhatsApp as the primary interface.

The initial business model will focus on selling the service to turf owners rather than charging customers directly.

---

# 2. Business Model Hypothesis

The initial hypothesis is:

> Turf owners are willing to pay for a system that saves them time, reduces fake bookings, automates booking management, and provides a simple WhatsApp-based business assistant.

This hypothesis must be validated through direct conversations with turf owners in Kolhapur.

The product should not invest heavily in infrastructure or advanced features before this hypothesis is validated.

---

# 3. Primary Customer

The primary customer is:

> Football turf owner or turf business operator.

The customer may own:

- One football turf
- Multiple football turfs
- A football academy
- A sports facility
- A multi-sport facility

The initial target is single-location football turf businesses.

---

# 4. End User

The end user is the person booking the turf.

The end user does not necessarily pay for the software.

The end user pays the turf business for the turf booking.

Example:

Customer
    │
    │ Pays ₹800 for turf booking
    ▼
Turf Business

Turf Business
    │
    │ Pays software subscription
    ▼
Turf AI Booking

The software business and turf booking transaction are separate.

# 6. Alternative Revenue Models

The following models may be evaluated during validation.

---

## 6.1 Monthly Subscription

Example:

₹999/month per turf business.

Advantages:

- Predictable recurring revenue
- Simple pricing
- Easy for owners to understand
- No impact on customer booking price

Disadvantages:

- Turf owner pays even during low booking periods
- Requires demonstrating ongoing value

---

## 6.2 Per Booking Fee

Example:

₹5–₹20 per successful booking.

Advantages:

- Owner pays based on usage
- Easy to start with low upfront commitment
- Revenue grows with turf activity

Disadvantages:

- Revenue is less predictable
- Requires tracking every successful booking
- May discourage owners from using the system for high booking volumes

---

## 6.3 Percentage of Booking

Example:

1%–5% of booking value.

Advantages:

- Revenue grows with turf revenue
- Low fixed cost for turf owners

Disadvantages:

- More complex payment accounting
- May be perceived as expensive
- Requires transparent transaction tracking

---

## 6.4 Hybrid Model

Example:

₹499/month
+
₹5 per successful booking.

Advantages:

- Small fixed recurring revenue
- Usage-based revenue
- Lower entry barrier

Disadvantages:

- More complicated pricing
- More difficult to communicate

---

The initial pilot should not immediately lock the product into a permanent pricing model.

The recommended strategy is:

Pilot
    ↓
Free or heavily discounted
    ↓
Real turf usage
    ↓
Measure value
    ↓
Collect owner feedback
    ↓
Test willingness to pay
    ↓

The first objective is product validation, not maximizing revenue.

# 8. Recommended Pilot Strategy

The first pilot should target:

> 1–3 football turf businesses in Kolhapur.

The pilot should be offered under one of the following arrangements:

### Option A — Free Pilot

Duration:

30 days

The turf owner uses the system for free.

The objective is to measure:

- Number of bookings
- Time saved
- Customer adoption
- Payment completion rate
- Owner satisfaction

---

### Option B — Free Setup + Paid Usage

The setup is free.

The turf owner pays a small amount per successful booking.

This can validate whether owners are willing to pay based on usage.

---

### Option C — Discounted Early-Access Pricing

Example:

Normal price:

₹1,999/month

Early adopter price:

₹499–₹999/month

The discounted price remains active for early customers for a fixed period.

# 9. Recommended Validation Approach

Before investing heavily in production infrastructure, speak to at least:

> 5–10 turf owners in Kolhapur.

Ask questions such as:

1. How do you currently manage bookings?
2. How many bookings do you receive per day?
3. Do customers usually contact you through WhatsApp?
4. How much time do you spend responding to booking messages?
5. Do you face double-booking issues?
6. How often do customers cancel?
7. How often do customers fail to show up?
8. Do you currently collect advance payments?
9. Would automatic payment confirmation be useful?
10. Would you use a WhatsApp AI assistant to manage bookings?
11. Would you allow customers to book without talking to you?
12. Would you pay for such a system?
13. Would you prefer monthly pricing or per-booking pricing?
14. What price would feel reasonable?

The goal is to understand the owner's actual workflow before finalizing the business model.

# 12. Pilot Success Criteria

A pilot should be considered successful if:

- At least 1 turf owner actively uses the system

# 13. Unit Economics

The business must track the cost associated with each turf business.

Potential costs include:

- WhatsApp messaging
- AI API usage
- Backend hosting
- Database hosting
- n8n infrastructure
- Payment gateway fees
- File/report storage
- Monitoring
- Support

The basic calculation is:

Monthly Revenue per Turf
    -
Monthly Infrastructure Cost per Turf
    =
Gross Contribution

Example:

Software Subscription:
₹999/month

Estimated infrastructure allocation:
₹200/month

Gross contribution:
₹799/month

Actual numbers must be calculated after real usage data is collected.

---

# 14. AI Cost Strategy

AI should be used efficiently.

The AI should not perform tasks that can be handled by deterministic backend logic.

For example:

The AI should understand:

> "Book tomorrow at 7 PM."

The backend should determine:

- Whether the slot exists
- Whether the slot is available
- Whether the customer is authorized
- Whether payment is required
- Whether the booking conflicts with another booking

Architecture:

AI
    ↓
Tool Call
    ↓
Spring Boot
    ↓
Business Logic
    ↓
PostgreSQL

This reduces AI cost and improves reliability.

---

# 15. WhatsApp Cost Strategy

WhatsApp messaging costs should be considered part of the operating cost.

The exact cost depends on:

- Meta's current pricing
- Message category
- Message volume
- Customer conversations
- Template usage
- Third-party provider fees, if applicable

The MVP should initially use the official WhatsApp Business Platform / Cloud API where appropriate.

Before finalizing commercial pricing, the actual WhatsApp cost per active turf should be measured.

---

# 16. Payment Gateway Cost Strategy

The payment gateway will generally charge transaction-related fees.

The system should track:

- Gross booking value
- Payment gateway fee
- Refund amount
- Net amount
- Turf owner's settlement

Example:

Booking:

₹800

Payment gateway fee:

₹X

Refund:

₹0

Net settlement:

₹800 - ₹X

The exact gateway fee should be based on the selected provider's current pricing.

The payment gateway fee should not be confused with the software subscription revenue.

# 20. Financial Principles

The product should follow these principles:

### Principle 1: Keep Initial Costs Low

Do not pay for infrastructure before validating the product.

### Principle 2: Validate Willingness to Pay

A turf owner using the product for free is not enough validation.

The product should eventually demonstrate willingness to pay.

### Principle 3: Keep Pricing Simple

Turf owners should easily understand how much they pay.

### Principle 4: Avoid Hidden Fees

All software fees should be clearly communicated.

### Principle 5: Separate Software and Payment Costs

The owner should clearly understand:

Software Fee
+
Payment Gateway Fee
+
WhatsApp/Infrastructure Cost (if passed on)

### Principle 6: Scale Costs with Revenue

Infrastructure costs should grow more slowly than revenue.
